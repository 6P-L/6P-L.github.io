# Transcript — P9 孙建国 Sun Jianguo

**W1. Tell me a bit about what you're studying for right now and what's riding on it.**

I'm preparing for the MBA 管理类联考 in December: math, logic, writing, and English 二. I'm 34 and I've been a hardware engineer for eleven years. I lead a team now, but in my company if you don't move to the management track by around 35, you basically stay an engineer forever. So the MBA is how I move up. To be honest there's also a face element. My wife knows, my parents know, a few colleagues know. Failing an exam at my age is not something I want to explain at Spring Festival.

**W2. Walk me through what a typical study day looked like yesterday or the last day you studied, from when you sat down to when you stopped.**

Yesterday was Friday, so a weeknight. Got home around 7:30, had dinner, played with my son, and he was asleep a bit after nine. I sat down at the dining table around 9:15 with the 联考 math workbook and a pencil. I spent maybe the first ten minutes flipping pages trying to decide what to do, because I had homework from the Saturday class but I also knew my 数据分析 (probability) is weak. I ended up doing about twelve problems on 应用题, got four wrong, marked them with a red pen, and stopped around 10:40 because I have a 9 am meeting and I was tired. Saturday and Sunday are different; those are the class days, eight hours each, fully scheduled.

**W3. What tools, apps, materials or people were involved in that day?**

The workbook, a pencil, a red pen, my paper notebook where I copy wrong problems. The 社科赛斯 app, but only to check the homework list and re-watch a replay sometimes. WeChat, because the class group posts the answers and my tutor sends me problems there. My tutor, Teacher Li, for math, but not yesterday; he's Wednesdays. No AI. I use Doubao at work to polish emails in English sometimes, that's it.

**Q1. Think about the last time you stepped away from a subject for a few days. What happened when you came back to it? How did you figure out where you were?**

Writing. I didn't touch 论证有效性分析 for almost two weeks in August because we had a product launch at work and I was travelling to the factory in Dongguan. When I came back, honestly I didn't figure it out by myself. The Saturday class was on writing that weekend so the teacher just picked it up where the syllabus was, and I followed. I looked at my notebook and saw my old essays with the teacher's red marks, and that reminded me I always miss the "causal fallacy" type. So the class schedule is what resets me. If there's no class, I probably drift to math, because math I can at least measure.

**Q2. What, if anything, keeps track of what you've already learned, what you got wrong and what's next? How well does that work?**

My paper 错题本, the class syllabus, and Teacher Li's memory, in that order. The syllabus tells me what's next in the big picture, and that works fine. The 错题本 works for recording, but I don't review it systematically; I flip through it when I feel guilty. Teacher Li knows my weak areas because he sees my work every week, that's the best tracker I have. The gap is weeknights. On a Tuesday at 9:15 I don't know whether I should do probability or go back to geometry. I just pick something, and sometimes I pick wrong. But I wouldn't call that a big crisis, it's just some wasted time.

**Q3. Tell me about the last time your study plan fell apart or you lost momentum. What caused it, and what brought you back, if anything did?**

That August launch again. For about ten days I did nothing on weeknights; I was getting home at eleven. Then my son was sick for a few days after that. What brought me back was simple: the Saturday class. I paid ¥19,800, I'm not going to skip it, and once I'm in the classroom I'm back in rhythm. Also my wife asked me once, "Are you still doing the MBA thing?" That was enough. I don't lack motivation. I lack hours. No app is going to give me hours.

**Q4. Describe the last time you got stuck on a concept or problem. What did you do, step by step? How did you feel about how it went?**

Wednesday before last, a probability problem with 不放回抽样 (sampling without replacement). I tried it twice, got two different answers, looked at the answer key and the key only gave the final number. I took a photo and sent it to Teacher Li on WeChat. He replied the next morning with a voice message explaining it, then at our session he gave me three similar problems. I felt fine about it. It cost me a night's wait, but I got a correct explanation from someone who knows the exam. My colleague once showed me an AI doing a logic problem; it got the reasoning wrong and sounded 100% sure. If I'd been learning from that, I'd have learned the wrong thing and not known it. Waiting until morning is better than being confidently wrong.

**Q5. When you use AI assistants for studying, what do you actually ask them for? What happens after you get the response?**

For studying, basically nothing. I tried once in July, asked Doubao to explain a 逻辑 question on 假言推理, and the explanation was long and I couldn't tell if it was right, so I asked the class group instead. That's the problem: to check the AI you need to already know the answer, and if I already knew, I wouldn't ask. At work I use it for email drafts because I can read the email and judge it myself. For exam content I can't judge, so I don't trust it. Maybe for English vocabulary it would be okay, but I have a word book for that.

**Q6. Walk me through how you move between your different study tools and materials in a session. What's that like?**

It's not complicated. Workbook, pencil, answer key, 错题本. Sometimes I open WeChat to check what the teacher assigned or to send a photo to Teacher Li, and then of course I see work messages, and that's the real distraction, not the switching. On weekends the teacher controls everything. I don't really feel a problem here. I'm an engineer; I don't need five apps to do math problems. Honestly, fewer tools is better for me, I don't want to learn new software at 9 pm.

**Q7. What materials are you studying from, and what do you do with them digitally, if anything? How do you feel about putting them into online tools?**

社科赛斯 handouts, their workbook, the 真题 (past papers) book, the 老吕 math book my tutor recommended, and my own notebooks. Digitally I do almost nothing: photos of problems to send to the tutor, and replays in the class app. Privacy doesn't worry me, it's exam prep, not company drawings. I just don't see the point of uploading. What would I get? The material is already organized by the class. Scanning pages would be one more job for me on a weeknight.

**Q8. Has anyone or anything ever checked in on your studying: a person, group, app or service? How did that go?**

Teacher Li, every week. That works because I'm paying him ¥400 an hour and he'll see immediately if I haven't done the problems; it's a bit embarrassing to show up unprepared at my age. The class has a 班主任 (class coordinator) who posts in the WeChat group, "Remember to submit homework," and I mostly ignore those because they're sent to 200 people. The 社科赛斯 app sends push notifications; I turned them off in the first week. So a person I have a relationship with, yes. Generic reminders, no, they're noise.

**Q9. If you think about the hardest part of preparing for this exam or course, what is it? Why is that the hardest part?**

Time, first. I get maybe 75 minutes on weeknights, and that's if my son sleeps on time. Second is math: I did 高数 in university but that was thirteen years ago, and 联考 math is fast, tricky problems, not calculus. So the hard part is using those short weeknight blocks on the right thing. If I spend 40 minutes on geometry when my real problem is probability, that's a whole evening wasted, and I only have maybe fifty evenings left before December. Writing structure is also hard, but that I leave to the class teacher.

**Q10. What have you spent money on for this exam or course so far, including courses, apps, tutors and subscriptions? How do you feel about each of those purchases now?**

¥19,800 for the 社科赛斯 weekend class. Teacher Li, ¥400 an hour, one to one-and-a-half hours a week since June, so roughly ¥8,000 to ¥9,000 so far. Books, maybe ¥600. The class app is included. No app subscriptions, zero. The class I feel okay about; the structure is good, though some sessions are too basic for me. Teacher Li I feel very good about, he's the one who actually moved my math score in the mock exams. The books are fine, necessary.

**C1. Of everything you've paid for so far, which felt most worth it and which felt least worth it, and why?**

Most worth it is Teacher Li, clearly. He's accountable, he knows me, and my mock math went from about 90 to about 120 out of 150 since June. Least worth it... some of the class. Maybe a third of the Saturday sessions are for people with weaker foundations, and I'm sitting there for three hours on things I know. But I'd still buy it again because the syllabus and the pressure of the schedule are what keep me moving, and colleagues who got into 北大光华 and 中大岭南 took the same class. I don't regret spending money. I regret wasting time.

**C2. If something could take one problem you've described today completely off your plate, which problem would you pick? What would that be worth to you per month, and what would you stop paying for or stop doing to make room for it?**

The weeknight problem: sitting down at 9:15 and knowing immediately which weak topic to spend tonight's 40 minutes on, with the right problems ready. Money isn't the issue; if it really saved me those evenings, it's worth a lot. But realistically, the way I'd solve it is ask Teacher Li to send me a short plan for each weeknight, and I'd pay him an extra hour a week, about ¥1,600 a month, and cut one Sunday elective session or just absorb it. For software doing the same job, today I'd pay ¥0. Not because ¥99 is expensive, ¥99 is nothing. It's because I don't trust it to pick the right topic, and if it picks wrong, I lose the evening, which is the thing I actually care about. If Teacher Li or someone who got into a good program told me "use this, it works," I might try it free first.

**Z1. [Concept read: "An AI study system that works from your own materials, remembers your progress across sessions, guides you step by step rather than giving answers, and reaches out to keep you on track."] What's your honest reaction? What would make this useless to you?**

Honestly, it's not for me, I think it's for students. "Guides you step by step rather than giving answers" sounds slow, I have 75 minutes, I'd rather have a correct worked solution and do more problems. "Works from your own materials" means I have to upload and scan, and that's the part I would never do on a weeknight. "Reaches out to keep you on track," I already turned off the class app's notifications, so that goes straight to mute. The "remembers my progress" part is the only thing that touches my real problem, but only if it tells me something correct about my weak topics for the 联考 specifically. What makes it useless: one confidently wrong explanation in math or logic, and I delete it that night. Also useless if it needs setup, or if no human teacher stands behind the content. If my tutor used it to track me and he checked it, maybe. By itself, no.
