# Transcript — P4 王浩 Wang Hao

*Interview conducted late September 2026, by video call. Participant sat the 客观题 exam about two weeks ago and is in the final stretch before the 主观题 exam in October. He asked at the start to keep it under 40 minutes.*

**W1. Tell me a bit about what you're studying for right now and what's riding on it.**

法考, the national judicial exam. I sat the objective part two weeks ago, and my estimated score from checking answers in the WeChat groups is around the line, a little above if the estimates are right, so I'm treating it as passed and grinding for the 主观题 in October. What's riding on it is pretty much everything, honestly. I quit a sales job eight months ago, I'm living on savings, and I've already burned through more than half of it between rent and the course. If I don't pass this year, I either go back to sales with a gap on my CV or I spend another year and I don't really have the money for another year. So, yeah, the stakes aren't small. I'd rather not dwell on it too much, to be honest.

**W2. Walk me through what a typical study day looked like yesterday or the last day you studied, from when you sat down to when you stopped.**

Yesterday was a normal day. I sat down at about 7:30, did an hour of 背诵 on 刑法 主观题 answer templates, the ones the 厚大 teacher gives you, out loud, with my cards. Then 9 to 12 was the teacher's 主观题 lecture on the app plus annotating the printed 讲义. After lunch I did one full 主观题 case, 民法 plus 民诉 combined, handwritten, timed at about 70 minutes, then compared it against the reference answer and marked where I lost points. Evening was 真题 review and then re-reading my 民法 notes, which is my weak spot. I stopped around 10:30. It's roughly 10 hours, most days look like that.

**W3. What tools, apps, materials or people were involved in that day?**

The 厚大 app for the lectures, the printed 讲义 — I have eight binders, one colour per subject — and the 真题 books. Handwritten 背诵 cards, a stack of maybe six or seven hundred now. For the objective part I used 觉晓's free question bank a lot, less now since the subjective part is writing. Occasionally DeepSeek, but only for things like "what's the difference between these two terms", and I don't trust it for anything I'd memorise. And a friend who's a lawyer quizzes me on weekends, mostly over WeChat voice. That's it. It's mostly paper, honestly.

**Q1. Think about the last time you stepped away from a subject for a few days. What happened when you came back to it? How did you figure out where you were?**

I don't really step away for a few days, the schedule doesn't allow it. But the real version of this problem for me is months, not days. I did 民法 in January and February, then the course moved on to 刑法, 刑诉, 行政法, and by around June I opened 民法 again for a mock and it was bad, like I recognised everything but couldn't produce anything. I figured out where I was the crude way: I did a set of 民法 真题 and looked at which ones I got wrong, then went back to those sections of the 讲义. It took about a week I hadn't planned for, which pushed everything else back. So the problem isn't "where was I", I always know where I am in the syllabus, it's "what has leaked out of my head since then".

**Q2. What, if anything, keeps track of what you've already learned, what you got wrong and what's next? How well does that work?**

"What's next" is the 厚大 syllabus, full stop, I don't need anything for that. For what I got wrong, I have a 错题本, a notebook, plus 觉晓 keeps an error list for the objective questions, which is fine but it's only for their question bank, it doesn't know anything about my 讲义. What I've learned — nothing really tracks that. My 背诵 cards are sorted into rubber-banded piles by how confident I am, which works okay but I'm the one deciding, and I'm probably overconfident on some. Honestly, it works well enough for "next" and badly for "what am I forgetting". I only find out I've forgotten something when I get it wrong.

**Q3. Tell me about the last time your study plan fell apart or you lost momentum. What caused it, and what brought you back, if anything did?**

It doesn't fall apart the way you might mean. I don't have a motivation problem, I have a savings problem, that's motivation enough. The closest was that week in June with 民法 I told you about — it wasn't that I stopped studying, it was that the plan was wrong and I had to steal a week from 行政法. The other time was in spring when I spent, I don't know, maybe a week and a half trying out apps. Three different things, one AI tutor, one planner app and one flashcard app. I set them all up, imported stuff, and at the end of it I'd lost time and deleted all three. What brought me back was just going back to the 讲义. That's sort of my lesson — every time I leave the teacher's structure, I lose time.

**Q4. Describe the last time you got stuck on a concept or problem. What did you do, step by step? How did you feel about how it went?**

Last week, a 民诉 point in a 主观题 case, whether a third party should be joined as 有独立请求权的第三人 or not. First I reread the section in the 讲义. That didn't settle it, so I rewatched the part of the lecture, about 15 minutes. Then I checked the reference answer for the 真题, which gave the conclusion but not really the reasoning. In the end I put it in the 厚大 student WeChat group and one of the 助教 answered the next morning. It was fine, but I lost most of an evening waiting. I did not ask an AI, because of what happened with 刑法.

*[Interviewer: What happened with 刑法?]*

In March I asked an AI about the 共犯 rules for a specific scenario, and it answered very confidently, with a whole structure. I wrote it on a card. Two weeks later the teacher said basically the opposite in lecture. If he hadn't happened to cover that exact point, I'd have walked into the exam with a wrong rule memorised. That scared me more than it probably should have, but on this exam one point can be the difference.

**Q5. When you use AI assistants for studying, what do you actually ask them for? What happens after you get the response?**

Now, very narrow things. Like "explain this term in plain language", or "give me five examples of 表见代理" just to get my head around it. Sometimes I paste my 主观题 answer and ask it to check if my writing is clear, not whether the law is right. After I get the response, I always check it against the 讲义, and if it contradicts the 讲义 I throw it away. So in practice it's a sort of dictionary. Maybe two or three times a week, I wouldn't say more. I don't use it to learn law, because I can't tell when it's wrong, and that's exactly the situation where you need to be able to tell.

**Q6. Walk me through how you move between your different study tools and materials in a session. What's that like?**

Honestly, it's not a big pain point. The lecture's on my iPad, the 讲义 is open on the table, I've got a pen. When I switch to 真题 it's the book. The phone is only for 觉晓 and WeChat, and I try to keep it face down. I guess the annoying part is that my errors are in three places — my 错题本, 觉晓, and notes in the margins of the 讲义 — and there's no single view of them. But I wouldn't say switching costs me much. The paper is the point, I remember things better when I've written them.

**Q7. What materials are you studying from, and what do you do with them digitally, if anything? How do you feel about putting them into online tools?**

The 厚大 讲义 and 真题 books, and the lecture videos. Digitally, basically nothing — I photograph a page sometimes to send to my friend. Uploading them somewhere, I'm careful. The PDF versions are watermarked with my name and phone number, and the course terms say you can't distribute them. I've heard of people in the groups getting their accounts frozen for sharing. So if I upload them to some app and that app gets them leaked, or it becomes a searchable thing, it's my name on it. I'm not saying never, but I'd need to be sure it's only for me and doesn't go anywhere. And I'm not photographing 800 pages of 讲义 by hand, I don't have the time.

**Q8. Has anyone or anything ever checked in on your studying: a person, group, app or service? How did that go?**

My friend on weekends, he quizzes me for about an hour, that's genuinely useful because he's a lawyer and he'll push back on my reasoning. The 厚大 course has a 班主任 who sends messages in the group, "打卡" reminders, study plans. I ignore most of them, I'm ahead of what they're asking. The planner app I tried in spring sent me notifications, I turned them off on day two. I don't need to be reminded to study. If something checked in on me, it would need to be about *what* I'm forgetting, not *whether* I'm studying. The second kind just annoys me.

**Q9. If you think about the hardest part of preparing for this exam or course, what is it? Why is that the hardest part?**

Two things, and they're connected. First, the volume — eight subjects, and by the time you finish the last one the first one is fading. 民法 is the one that keeps me up at night, because it's the biggest part of the 主观题 and I studied it the earliest. Second, the 主观题 writing itself — you can know the law and still lose points because you didn't hit the scoring keywords the graders want. Nobody grades my practice answers except me against the reference answer, and I'm not a great judge of my own answers. The volume is the hardest because there's no shortcut, you just have to keep re-feeding everything back into your head, and there's never enough time to re-feed all of it.

**Q10. What have you spent money on for this exam or course so far, including courses, apps, tutors and subscriptions? How do you feel about each of those purchases now?**

The 厚大 full-year course, ¥12,800. That's the big one, and I'm fine with it, it's the backbone of everything. 真题 books and some extra printed materials, maybe ¥600, and printing, a few hundred more. I bought a 主观题 批改 add-on, a paid package of essay grading, about ¥1,500 — mixed feelings, the feedback comes back slow and it's a bit generic. Apps — in spring one of them I paid a monthly membership for, ¥30-something, and I deleted it within two weeks, so that was a waste, though a small one. DeepSeek and 觉晓 are free. No tutor, my friend does it for free, I buy him dinner.

**C1. Of everything you've paid for so far, which felt most worth it and which felt least worth it, and why?**

Most worth it is the course, not even close. It's the plan, the teacher is the authority, and everyone in the groups is on the same materials, so the information is shared. Least worth it is the app membership in spring, not because of the money but because of the week I lost setting it up. That's the real cost for me — time. The 批改 package is in the middle; it forced me to write full answers on a schedule, which was good, but I don't think the feedback raised my score much. I'd pay again for something that clearly raises the 主观题 score. I would not pay again for anything that needs me to rebuild my system around it.

**C2. If something could take one problem you've described today completely off your plate, which problem would you pick? What would that be worth to you per month, and what would you stop paying for or stop doing to make room for it?**

The forgetting. Knowing exactly what from 民法 is leaking out and making me review that and only that, at the right time — that's the one. What would it be worth... in principle, a ¥99 a month thing is nothing compared to ¥12,800 if it actually works. But I have to be honest: right now, with three weeks left, I'd pay ¥0, because I'm not going to change my system three weeks before the exam, no way. If I have to retake next year, and it was proven — like, people in my groups passing with it, and it runs on the 厚大 讲义 without errors — then maybe ¥39 a month during the peak months, June to October, and I wouldn't pay in the off months. To make room I'd drop the 批改 package next year, that's the only thing I'd cut; I'm not cutting the course. Really I'm spending savings, so every yuan is coming out of rent.

**Z1. [Read neutral one-line description: "An AI study system that works from your own materials, remembers your progress across sessions, guides you step by step rather than giving answers, and reaches out to keep you on track."] What's your honest reaction? What would make this useless to you?**

Mixed. "Works from your own materials" and "remembers your progress" — that's the part that's interesting, that's the 民法 problem. "Guides you step by step rather than giving answers" — honestly, I don't want that, I have a teacher, I don't need an AI teaching me law in a different way from him; if anything that's dangerous for me. "Reaches out to keep you on track" — I'd turn that off, same as the planner app. What would make it useless: one wrong legal rule, and I'm done, I'd delete it the same day. Also if I have to upload my 讲义 and I'm not sure where it goes, or if its structure doesn't match 厚大's chapters, or if the setup takes more than an evening. And, sorry, but if it's not proven by people who've actually passed, I'm not trying it in peak season. Maybe in the off-season, if I'm retaking.
